### SEC-3 · Regulatory incident clocks and mandatory AI records (EU, India, US)  — **P1** · THIN · Section I · extend Turn 90 (cross-link Turns 78 and 96)

*Overlaps gap doc:* none. It cross-links SEC-1 (CRA reporting lane), FDE-11 (records retention, the other half of the retention policy) and gap-doc #1's vulnerability pipeline.

**What it is.** A single runbook that maps an AI incident to every legal reporting clock it can start. It also covers the minimum record-retention rules, which pull against zero data retention and data minimisation.

**Why now.** The clocks that most EU and India deployments hit are already law. GDPR gives 72 hours to report a personal-data breach ([EUR-Lex, GDPR Art. 33](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)). CERT-In gives 6 hours ([CERT-In, 28 Apr 2022](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)). A bank's IT vendor must report fast enough for the bank to meet RBI's 6-hour deadline ([Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/RBI-Releases-Master-Direction-To-Regulate-Outsourcing-of-IT-Services)). Since 11 Sep 2026 the CRA has added 24 h and 72 h clocks for AI products shipped into the EU ([European Commission, accessed 26 Sep 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)). AI Act Art. 73 reporting follows once the high-risk rules apply: 2 Dec 2027 for Annex III and 2 Aug 2028 for Annex I, under the Digital Omnibus on AI (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) ([European Commission, accessed 26 Sep 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)). Retention floors pull the other way. CERT-In requires 180 days of ICT logs. California's automated-decision employment rules require 4 years of decision data ([Mayer Brown, 2 Sep 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/california-adopts-new-employment-ai-regulations-effective-october-1-2025)). SEBI has proposed at least 5 years for AI/ML inputs and outputs, in a consultation that is still not finalised ([SEBI, 20 Jun 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2025/1750415065695.pdf)).

**What Vol 2 has today.** Turn 90 teaches AI incident types and on-call levers (Q447: "Silent model changes, hallucination incidents at scale, prompt-injection waves, poisoned sources, cost runaways, data leaks"; Q448). Q405 lists HIPAA "breach notification", the topic index maps DORA to Turn 82, and Turn 96 Q478 treats trace retention only as something to limit. No reporting clocks and no retention floors.

**What to teach.**
- Build a clock matrix for each engagement. For each regime, record the trigger, the deadline, what starts the clock, who decides and who files. Start points differ: awareness (GDPR), classification (DORA's 4 h), a materiality determination (SEC), or the vendor's own detection (RBI).
- As a vendor to Indian banks, write the contract clause and the on-call runbook together, so the bank gets your report in time for its 6-hour RBI filing. Check whether the incident is a CERT-In Annexure I type, keep 180 days of ICT logs, and keep system clocks synchronised.
- As a manufacturer shipping AI software into the EU, add a CRA lane to the runbook. Name who decides that a vulnerability is "actively exploited", who files on ENISA's SRP, and how users are told (SEC-1).
- Write one two-sided retention policy per data class, with legal floors (CERT-In 180 days, California's 4 years, SEBI's proposed 5 years) on one side and minimisation and provider ZDR on the other. The provider can run with ZDR while the deployer keeps redacted, access-controlled records. Teach this once, together with FDE-11.
- Prepare report templates and evidence packs (traces, model and prompt versions) in advance. AI Act Art. 73 allows an incomplete first report, so the goal is to file on time.
- Track pending changes separately from law. The Digital Omnibus 96-hour proposal is not in force. MeitY floated shortening the 18-month DPDP window to 12 months for Significant Data Fiduciaries in Jan 2026 ([Business Standard, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html)); as of 27 Sep 2026 no amending notification had been published, so the 12- and 18-month clocks in Rule 1 of G.S.R. 846(E) still stand ([tracker, checked 6 Sep 2026](https://dpdprules.org/timeline)).

**Idea to remember.** An AI incident is also a legal event. Know which clocks it starts (6 h in India, 4 h/24 h under DORA, 24 h under NIS2 and the CRA, 72 h under GDPR), what starts each clock, and which records the law makes you keep.

**Interview questions.**
1. An Indian bank's AI assistant, which your company runs for the bank, leaks customer data through a prompt-injection attack. Which clocks start? — CERT-In needs a report within 6 hours of noticing, because data breaches and leaks are Annexure I incident types. As the bank's IT vendor, you must tell the bank without undue delay so it can report to RBI within 6 hours of your detection. Once the DPDP breach rules apply (May 2027), the Data Protection Board must be told without delay, with a detailed report within 72 hours. The runbook should name who decides and who files.
2. Attackers are actively exploiting a prompt-injection flaw in your self-hosted agent product, which EU banks run. Which clocks start? — Under the CRA you are the manufacturer: an early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours and a final report 14 days after the fix, all through ENISA's SRP, and you must inform users. The bank has its own DORA clock if it classifies the incident as major (within 4 hours of classification and no later than 24 hours from awareness), plus GDPR's 72 hours if personal data was breached. The 96-hour single entry point is only a proposal.
3. How do you reconcile zero data retention with rules that set retention floors? — Split the layers. The model provider can run with zero retention while the deployer keeps its own redacted, access-controlled inputs, outputs and decisions for the required period: 180 days of logs for CERT-In, 4 years for California's automated-decision employment rules, and the 5 years SEBI has proposed. Document the retention basis for each data class.

### SEC-1 · EU Cyber Resilience Act and revised Product Liability Directive for AI software  — **P2** · MISSING · Section H · new turn after Turn 82 (one turn for both laws; the horizon lens's HOR-9 proposes the same pair, so keep only one)

*Overlaps gap doc:* #1: for vendors that ship products into the EU, an actively exploited vulnerability in an AI product starts a CRA clock, so #1's vulnerability pipeline needs a reporting lane (standalone SaaS and own-use builds are out of scope). #4: the regulation map leaves out EU product law, which applies whatever the product's AI Act risk class.

**What it is.** Two EU product laws that now reach AI software. The CRA (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) sets secure-by-design, vulnerability-handling (including an SBOM) and incident-reporting duties for "products with digital elements" placed on the EU market. The revised PLD (Directive (EU) 2024/2853) makes software, including AI systems, a "product" under no-fault liability for harm to natural persons.

**Why now.** CRA Art. 14 reporting has applied since 11 Sep 2026, including to in-scope products placed on the market before full application on 11 Dec 2027 (Arts 69(3) and 71(2)). Fines reach EUR 15m or 2.5% of worldwide turnover (Art. 64(2)) ([EUR-Lex, Reg. (EU) 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng)). ENISA's SRP has been "operational as of 11 September 2026" ([European Commission, accessed 26 Sep 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)). Scope is narrower than many assume. The Commission's FAQ says "placing on the market is considered not to take place where a product is manufactured for one's own use". It also says that "standalone Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) or other cloud solutions ... are not themselves products with digital elements" unless they are remote data processing for a product. Tailor-made software built for one business user is in scope ([European Commission CRA FAQ v1.4, 4 Sep 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/cyber-resilience-act-implementation-frequently-asked-questions)). The PLD applies to products placed on the market or put into service after 9 Dec 2026, which is also the transposition deadline (Arts 2(1) and 22). It covers software "irrespective of whether the software is stored on a device, accessed through a communication network or cloud technologies, or supplied through a software-as-a-service model" (recital 13). Only natural persons can claim, and property and data used for professional purposes are excluded (Arts 5 and 6) ([EUR-Lex, Dir. (EU) 2024/2853](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj/eng)). Hungary (16 Dec 2025), Croatia (7 Jul 2026) and Lithuania (Act XV-1080, adopted 25 Jun 2026) have already transposed it, according to law-firm trackers ([Reed Smith, 2026](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/eu-product-liability-directive-where-things-stand-with-member-state-transposition/)). The separate AI Liability Directive was withdrawn (notice in OJ C/2025/5423, 6 Oct 2025) ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52025XC05423)).

**What Vol 2 has today.** Nothing found after searching for: "Cyber Resilience", "CRA", "ENISA", "SBOM", "NIS2", "product liab", "defect", "Liability Directive". The closest are Turn 77 Q380 (AI-BOM: "A bill of materials listing models, adapters, datasets, engines, servers and their versions, hashes and licences") and liability only for vendor IP indemnities (Turn 85 Q423) and agent payments (Turn 70).

**What to teach.**
- Run the CRA scope test for each component, not for the product as a whole. Out of scope: a customer's own-use internal builds, and standalone SaaS. In scope: downloadable, on-prem and self-hosted software; agent SDKs and MCP servers distributed as packages; AI-enabled devices; remote data processing a product needs in order to work; and tailor-made software sold to one business customer. For tailor-made software, only secure-by-default configuration and free security updates can be agreed otherwise (FAQ 4.2.5).
- Build the CRA evidence before launch: a machine-readable SBOM (Annex I Part II(1)), a vulnerability-handling process, a declared support period and a reporting runbook for the 24 h / 72 h clocks (SEC-3). Extend the AI-BOM from Turn 77 rather than keeping two inventories.
- Know the AI Act link (CRA Art. 12). A high-risk AI system that meets the CRA's essential requirements is presumed to meet AI Act Art. 15 cybersecurity. The AI Act Art. 43 conformity procedure applies. By derogation, important (Annex III) or critical (Annex IV) products follow the CRA procedure for the cybersecurity requirements if they would need CRA third-party assessment and fall under the AI Act's internal-control procedure (Annex VI).
- Map PLD exposure by audience. Consumer-facing AI products carry most of it. B2B and employee-facing deployments carry little beyond personal injury, because only natural persons can claim, and property "used exclusively for professional purposes" (Art. 6(1)(b)(iii)) and data "used for professional purposes" (Art. 6(1)(c)) are excluded.
- Treat substantial modification as a manufacturer event. A substantial change, including one made through a software update or the system's continuous learning, can make the modifier the manufacturer ([Gibson Dunn, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-product-liability-directive-responding-to-software-ai-and-complex-supply-chains/)). Keep change records and evaluation evidence, because courts can order disclosure.
- Keep shipping security updates. Under Art. 11(2)(c), the "defect arose later" defence is lost when the defect comes from "a lack of software updates or upgrades necessary to maintain safety" that were within the manufacturer's control. Free and open-source software supplied outside a commercial activity is excluded (Art. 2(2)).

**Idea to remember.** EU product law reaches the AI software you ship, not software a customer builds for itself. Shipping into the EU now means a 24-hour CRA clock for exploited vulnerabilities, and from 9 Dec 2026 no-fault liability to consumers.

**Interview questions.**
1. Does the CRA apply to our LLM chatbot? — It depends on how it is delivered. Standalone SaaS is generally not a product with digital elements (NIS2 may apply instead) unless it is remote data processing for a product. Downloadable apps, on-prem or self-hosted software, SDKs and devices are in scope. Internal tools built by the customer for its own use are not placed on the market; bespoke software you sell to one customer still is.
2. How does the CRA relate to the AI Act for a high-risk AI system? — Under CRA Art. 12, meeting the CRA's essential requirements gives a presumption of conformity with AI Act Art. 15 cybersecurity, and the AI Act Art. 43 procedure applies. By derogation, important or critical products follow the CRA procedure for the cybersecurity requirements if they would need CRA third-party assessment and fall under the AI Act's internal-control procedure.
3. We fine-tune a vendor model and ship it in a consumer app in the EU. What does the new PLD change? — For products placed on the market after 9 Dec 2026, a substantial modification, including through updates or continuous learning, can make you the manufacturer. You can then be liable without fault to natural persons for death, injury, property damage or loss of non-professional data. Keep change records and evaluation evidence, and keep shipping security updates: a missing update that was within your control removes the "defect arose later" defence.

### SEC-4 · EU GPAI rules for downstream modifiers and the GPAI Code of Practice  — **P2** · THIN · Section H · extend Turn 79

*Overlaps gap doc:* none

**What it is.** Two questions for anyone who modifies a general-purpose AI (GPAI) model. First, when does fine-tuning or other modification make an enterprise a "GPAI provider" under the AI Act? Second, what does the voluntary Code of Practice ask of providers under Arts 53 and 55?

**Why now.** The Commission's non-binding GPAI guidelines (18 Jul 2025) give an indicative criterion. A downstream modifier becomes the provider only if the modification uses more than one-third of the original model's training compute. If that figure is unknown, the fallback is one-third of 10^23 FLOP (floating-point operations), or of 10^25 FLOP for systemic-risk models. Even above the line, only the modification has to be documented ([WilmerHale, 24 Jul 2025](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250724-european-commission-issues-guidelines-for-providers-of-general-purpose-ai-models)). The Code of Practice was published on 10 Jul 2025 with Transparency, Copyright, and Safety & Security chapters ([Jones Day, Aug 2025](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/08/eu-ai-act-european-commission-publishes-generalpurpose-ai-code-of-practice)). "From 2 August 2026, the AI Office and authorities of the Member States are responsible for ... enforcing the AI Act" ([European Commission, accessed 26 Sep 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)). Models placed on the market before 2 Aug 2025 must comply by 2 Aug 2027 ([European Commission GPAI FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/guidelines-obligations-general-purpose-ai-providers)).

**What Vol 2 has today.** Turn 79 says "GPAI model providers have their own duties", Q388 mentions "separate rules for general-purpose AI models", Q392 covers a deployer becoming a provider of a high-risk system by substantial modification, and Q420 says GPAI providers "must respect opt-outs and publish training-content summaries". Nothing covers when fine-tuning makes you a GPAI provider, the compute criterion or the Code of Practice.

**What to teach.**
- Estimate the compute of a fine-tune, distillation or continued pre-training run and compare it with the one-third indicative criterion (or the fallback thresholds). Record the estimate in the project file.
- Most LoRA and supervised fine-tunes stay far below the line. The team is still responsible for the AI system it builds, including Art. 50 transparency and any high-risk duties.
- If a modification does cross the line, document the modification only, not the whole base model.
- Collect the upstream provider's Art. 53 documentation for the customer's AI Act file. Treat a vendor's signature on the Code of Practice, or other evidence of compliance, as a due-diligence signal.
- Know the calendar: GPAI duties since 2 Aug 2025, enforcement since 2 Aug 2026, and 2 Aug 2027 for models placed on the market before 2 Aug 2025.

**Idea to remember.** Most enterprise fine-tunes use far less than one-third of the base model's training compute, so the fine-tuner does not become a GPAI provider. It still owns the AI system it builds, and it needs the upstream provider's documentation.

**Interview questions.**
1. Does LoRA fine-tuning of an open-weight model make us a GPAI provider under the EU AI Act? — Normally not. The Commission's indicative criterion, in non-binding guidelines, is whether the modification uses more than one-third of the original model's training compute. Even above that line, only the modification needs documenting. Your AI-system obligations (Art. 50 transparency, high-risk duties) still apply.
2. What is the GPAI Code of Practice, and why does it matter to a deployer? — A voluntary compliance tool published on 10 Jul 2025, with Transparency, Copyright, and Safety & Security chapters. Whether a vendor has signed it, or can otherwise show compliance, is a practical due-diligence signal and tells you what documentation to ask for.
3. What changed on 2 August 2026 for GPAI? — Enforcement started: the AI Office and Member State authorities became responsible for enforcing the AI Act, including the Commission's powers over GPAI providers. The obligations themselves had applied since 2 Aug 2025.

### SEC-5 · Automated-decision and AI-in-hiring rules beyond GDPR Art. 22 (US state and city rules, UK DUAA)  — **P2** · THIN · Section H · new turn after Turn 83 (cross-link Turn 81)

*Overlaps gap doc:* #4: of the automated-decision laws, the gap doc names only Colorado SB 26-189. Gap-doc #4 and the horizon lens's HOR-6 (CPPA ADMT in employment) should cross-reference it rather than repeat it.

**What it is.** The rules now in force for AI used in hiring and other "significant decisions". They include bias audits and notices (NYC Local Law 144), anti-discrimination liability that reaches vendors acting as the employer's "agent" (California CRD), notice and proxy bans (Illinois), pre-use notice, opt-out, access and appeal rights (California CPPA ADMT rules), Colorado's disclosure duties from 2027, and the UK's move away from a GDPR-style prohibition.

**Why now.** California's CRD regulations were approved on 27 Jun 2025 and took effect on 1 Oct 2025. Illinois HB 3773 (Public Act 103-0804) has applied since 1 Jan 2026 ([Morgan Lewis, Sep 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/09/illinois-passes-new-law-to-address-ai-in-the-workplace)), but the state's implementing rules on notice have been postponed ([Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/illinois-postpones-proposed-regulations-on-ai-in-employment/)). OAL approval of the CPPA regulations was announced on 23 Sep 2025, and they took effect on 1 Jan 2026. Under §7200(b), a business already using ADMT for a significant decision must comply by 1 Jan 2027 ([CPPA, 23 Sep 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html)). Colorado SB 26-189 (signed 14 May 2026) sets disclosure-focused ADMT duties from 1 Jan 2027, starting with developer documentation for deployers ([Colorado General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189)). A New York State Comptroller audit (2 Dec 2025) found that the city's enforcer, DCWP, identified 1 non-compliance issue among 32 companies, where the auditors found at least 17 potential instances. DCWP received only 2 complaints about automated employment tools between Jul 2023 and Jun 2025 ([NY State Comptroller, 2 Dec 2025](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools)). In the UK, SI 2026/82 (made 29 Jan 2026) brought DUAA s.80 into force on 5 Feb 2026 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/made)).

**What Vol 2 has today.** Turn 83 Q408 teaches the impact ratio and four-fifths rule, and Q411 lists "Blind inputs, rubric-based scoring, human review of borderline cases and re-testing on every release". Turn 81 Q399 covers GDPR Art. 22 safeguards, and Turn 79 Q391 lists employment as Annex III high-risk. None of the US hiring or ADMT laws appears, and the UK divergence is not mentioned.

**What to teach.**
- Start with the scope question: does AI make, or substantially replace, a significant decision (hiring, credit, housing, health care, education)? Most assistant, search and document builds do not, so most of these rules will not apply to them.
- Keep a checklist for each jurisdiction. NYC: independent bias audit, published summary and candidate notice. Illinois: notice, no discriminatory effect, no zip-code proxies. California CRD: the vendor can be liable as the employer's agent, records are kept 4 years, and anti-bias testing counts toward the defence. CPPA: pre-use notice, opt-out, access and appeal by 1 Jan 2027. Colorado: disclosure duties from 1 Jan 2027.
- Treat the four-fifths rule as a screening heuristic, not an enforced federal test. Its federal footing has eroded (EO 14281, 23 Apr 2025; DOJ Office of Legal Counsel opinion, 9 Jun 2026), but NYC audits still use impact ratios. Report impact ratios together with significance tests ([DOJ, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-concludes-eeoc-disparate-impact-guidelines-violate-constitution)).
- Contrast the UK and the EU. The UK's new Arts 22A-22D permit solely automated significant decisions with safeguards: information, the right to make representations, human intervention and the right to contest. Decisions based entirely or partly on special-category data stay restricted to explicit consent or specified legal bases ([UK government DUAA factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-use-and-access-act-2025-factsheets/data-use-and-access-act-factsheet-uk-gdpr-and-dpa)). The EU's Art. 22 is still a general prohibition with exceptions.
- Build the evidence pipeline: impact ratios with confidence intervals for every release, counterfactual swap tests, the audit summary, notice logs, and retained inputs, outputs and decisions for the legal period, all linked to model and prompt versions.

**Idea to remember.** In hiring and other significant decisions, several jurisdictions now require notices, bias testing, multi-year records and opt-out or appeal paths, and the vendor that builds the tool can share the liability.

CPPA ADMT rights apply to existing uses from 1 Jan 2027, and Colorado's duties start the same day.
2. How did the UK change the GDPR Article 22 model? — From 5 Feb 2026, DUAA s.80 lets organisations make solely automated significant decisions with safeguards: information, representations, human intervention and the right to contest. Decisions based on special-category data remain restricted to explicit consent or specified legal bases. The EU version is still a general prohibition with exceptions.
3. What evidence should a hiring-AI pipeline produce? — Impact ratios with confidence intervals for every release, counterfactual swap tests, the audit summary, notice logs, and retained inputs, outputs and decisions for the legally required period, all linked to model and prompt versions.

### SEC-6 · Gap doc #5 (deepened): Companion-AI, minors and age assurance  — **P2 (P1 for consumer products)** · MISSING · Section H · the gap-doc #5 turn: new turn after Turn 83 (cross-link Turn 26)

### SEC-7 · Deepfake and voice-clone fraud against identity verification (KYC, call centres, help desks)  — **P2** · THIN · Section H · new turn after Turn 78 (cross-link Turns 60 and 106)

*Overlaps gap doc:* none (distinct from #1, which is about AI finding vulnerabilities).

**What it is.** Defending onboarding, account recovery and payment approval against synthetic faces, voices and documents. The controls are: injection-attack detection alongside liveness checks and presentation-attack detection (PAD), device and camera attestation, call-back on trusted channels, step-up authentication, and a rule that a voice or video identity alone never authorises a high-risk action, whether a person or an AI voice agent takes it.

**Why now.** The US Treasury's FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network) reported a rise in suspicious-activity reports describing deepfake media, "particularly the use of fraudulent identity documents to circumvent identity verification and authentication methods" ([FinCEN FIN-2024-Alert004, 13 Nov 2024](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-issues-alert-fraud-schemes-involving-deepfake-media-targeting-financial)). In early 2024 an Arup employee was deceived on a video call with deepfaked senior staff, and the firm lost about US$25m; the case was disclosed in May 2024 ([CNN, 16 May 2024](https://edition.cnn.com/2024/05/16/tech/arup-deepfake-scam-loss-hong-kong-intl-hnk/index.html)). Deloitte projects that GenAI-enabled fraud losses in the US could reach US$40bn by 2027, up from US$12.3bn in 2023 ([Deloitte Center for Financial Services, 2024](https://www2.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/financial-services/financial-services-industry-predictions/2024/deepfake-banking-fraud-risk-on-the-rise.html)). FDEs are now building the voice agents and know-your-customer (KYC) flows that these attacks target.

**What Vol 2 has today.** Turn 106 Q527 asks for "out-of-band verification for sensitive requests" for voice cloning, Q528 says voiceprints used to identify people are biometric data, and Turn 60 Q297 lists voice-agent compliance points. Nothing covers KYC injection attacks, liveness or PAD, or deepfake-resistant authentication design (searched: "KYC", "liveness", "spoof", "deepfake").

**What to teach.**
- Threat-model onboarding, account recovery and payment approval against synthetic faces, voices and documents. Separate presentation attacks (something shown to a real camera) from injection attacks (synthetic video fed straight into the capture pipeline through a virtual camera or emulator).
- Pair liveness and PAD with injection-attack detection and device and camera integrity signals.
- Never let a voice or video identity alone authorise a high-risk action. Use possession or out-of-band factors (app push, call-back to a registered number), step-up authentication and transaction limits.
- Apply the same rule to AI voice agents. An agent that resets credentials or moves money needs the same out-of-band factors and anomaly detection as a human agent.
- Log decisions and signals for investigations in the style of suspicious-activity reports (SARs), and train help desks on FinCEN's red flags.

**Idea to remember.** A face or voice is no longer proof of identity. Pair liveness with injection-attack detection, and never let a voice or video identity alone authorise a high-risk action, whether a person or an AI agent carries it out.

**Interview questions.**
1. Your bank wants its AI voice agent to authenticate callers by voiceprint and then process transfers. What do you push back on? — Voice can be cloned, so a voiceprint should only lower friction. High-risk actions need possession or out-of-band factors (app push or call-back to a registered number), transaction limits and anomaly detection. Log everything for SAR-style investigation.
2. What is an injection attack in remote identity verification, and why doesn't liveness alone stop it? — The attacker feeds synthetic video straight into the capture pipeline (a virtual camera or emulator) instead of presenting something to a real camera. Presentation-attack detection only examines what the camera sees, so you also need device and camera integrity signals and injection detection.
3. What did FinCEN's November 2024 alert highlight? — A rise in suspicious-activity reports describing deepfake media, especially fraudulent identity documents used to get past identity verification and authentication.

### SEC-8 · Multi-tenant isolation and LLM side channels (prompt-cache timing, KV-cache sharing, token-length leaks)  — **P2** · THIN · Section H · extend Turn 74 (with a security note in Turn 31)

*Overlaps gap doc:* #16: the gap doc treats KV and prompt caches as a serving topic. This adds the side-channel threat model. Cache retention and isolation-scope facts live in MOD-7 (the deepened #16), and functional tenant isolation lives in FDE-10.

**What it is.** Performance features such as prefix and prompt caching, KV-cache (key-value cache) sharing, speculative decoding and token streaming create side channels. Timing and packet sizes can reveal other tenants' prompts or conversation topics, so shared LLM infrastructure needs cache-isolation scopes, cache salts, and padding or batching of streamed output.

**Why now.** Gu et al. (Stanford, ICML 2025) detected global prompt-cache sharing across users at seven API providers, OpenAI among them at the time ([arXiv 2502.07776, Feb 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07776)). Song et al. showed KV-cache timing leaks in serving systems (accepted to IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, TIFS) ([arXiv 2409.20002, v5 21 Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20002)), and Carlini and Nasr attacked speculative-decoding timing ([arXiv 2410.17175, Oct 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17175)). Microsoft's Whisper Leak inferred conversation topics from the sizes and timing of encrypted streamed packets, with above 98% AUPRC (area under the precision-recall curve) for many models. In response, OpenAI and Azure added an "obfuscation" field of random-length text, Mistral added a "p" parameter and xAI also mitigated; Microsoft says Azure's mitigation makes the attack "no longer a practical risk" ([Microsoft Security, 7 Nov 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/11/07/whisper-leak-a-novel-side-channel-cyberattack-on-remote-language-models/)). Mitigations now ship in products: vLLM offers a per-request cache_salt ([vLLM docs, accessed 26 Sep 2026](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/design/prefix_caching.html)). "Caches are also isolated per workspace within an organization on the Claude API, Claude Platform on AWS, and Microsoft Foundry; Bedrock and Google Cloud use organization-level isolation only" ([Anthropic docs, accessed 26 Sep 2026](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). A SaaS that serves many customers from one workspace therefore shares one cache across them.

**What Vol 2 has today.** Turn 74 says to "isolate data by user and tenant", Turn 50 Q246 lists vector-store multi-tenancy options, Turn 31 Q148 teaches prefix-cache hashing purely as a performance feature, Q438 says to "key caches by experiment arm", and Turn 133 names "side channels" only as a TEE limitation. Nothing covers cache-timing, token-length or cross-tenant cache leaks.

**What to teach.**
- The threat model: a cache hit returns measurably faster, so a shared cache leaks whether someone else sent the same prefix. Streamed packet sizes and timing leak topics. Speculative decoding leaks through timing.
- Scope every cache (prompt, KV and semantic) to the tenant. Put high-sensitivity tenants in separate workspaces or projects, set a per-tenant cache_salt in self-hosted vLLM, and keep tenant data out of shared cached prefixes. Check each provider's isolation scope; that fact is taught once, in MOD-7.
- For streaming, turn on the provider's obfuscation or padding options, batch tokens, or disable streaming for sensitive uses.
- Put cache scope, retention and side-channel mitigations in the architecture document, so the answer to the customer's CISO is written down.
- Keep the split across lenses: side channels here, functional tenant isolation in FDE-10, and cache retention and cost in MOD-7.

**Idea to remember.** Every cache is a shared resource. Scope prompt, KV and semantic caches to the tenant, or assume that timing can reveal what other tenants asked.

**Interview questions.**
1. How can prompt caching leak data between users? — A cached prefix returns measurably faster. If the cache is shared across users, an attacker can probe candidate prefixes and learn what others sent; a 2025 audit found such sharing at seven API providers. Scope caches per tenant and keep tenant-specific secrets out of shared prefixes.
2. You serve 40 enterprise customers from one API workspace with prompt caching on. What is the risk, and what is the fix? — They share one provider cache, and on Bedrock and Google Cloud the scope is the whole organisation. Put high-sensitivity tenants in separate workspaces or projects, add per-tenant salts in self-hosted engines (vLLM cache_salt), and don't put tenant data in cached system prefixes.
3. What is Whisper Leak? — A side channel Microsoft disclosed in Nov 2025: the sizes and timing of encrypted streamed tokens let a network observer classify conversation topics. OpenAI and Azure added a random-length "obfuscation" field, Mistral a "p" parameter, and xAI also mitigated. You can also batch tokens or avoid streaming for sensitive uses.

SEBI's 20 Jun 2025 consultation proposes client disclosure and at least 5 years' retention of AI/ML inputs and outputs, and no final guidelines were found as of Sep 2026 ([SEBI, 20 Jun 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/reports/jun-2025/consultation-paper-on-guidelines-for-responsible-usage-of-ai-ml-in-indian-securities-markets_94687.html)). **What Vol 2 has today.** India appears only as DPDP (Turn 81, Q400-401), and Turn 82 Q406 frames bank model-risk management in US SR 11-7 terms only. Nothing found after searching for: "RBI", "SEBI", "IRDAI", "FREE-AI", "MeitY", "CERT-In", "governance guidelines".

Treat this entry as P1 for Indian banks once RBI finalises the guidance.

### SEC-13 · Accessibility law for AI interfaces (EU EAA, US ADA Title II, India's SEBI digital-accessibility circulars)  — **P2** · THIN · Section H · extend Turn 83 (cross-link Turn 59 Agent User Interfaces)

*Overlaps gap doc:* none

**What it is.** Legal accessibility requirements, in practice WCAG 2.1 AA or the European standard EN 301 549, applied to customer-facing chat widgets, agent user interfaces and voice bots. The work includes screen-reader-friendly streaming (an aria-live region that does not announce every token), keyboard operation, accessible CAPTCHAs and age or identity checks, and non-voice alternatives for voice-only agents.

**Why now.** The European Accessibility Act (EAA) applies from 28 Jun 2025 to consumer-facing services such as banking and e-commerce, where customer chatbots now sit ([EUR-Lex, Dir. (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng); [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/union-equality-strategy-rights-persons-disabilities-2021-2030/european-accessibility-act_en)). Microenterprises providing services are exempt, and internal employee tools are not covered. In the US, a Department of Justice interim final rule (effective 20 Apr 2026) moved the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Title II web and app deadlines. State and local governments with a population of 50,000 or more move from 24 Apr 2026 to 26 Apr 2027. Smaller entities and special district governments move from 26 Apr 2027 to 26 Apr 2028. The WCAG 2.1 AA standard is unchanged ([Federal Register 2026-07663, 20 Apr 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/20/2026-07663/extension-of-compliance-dates-for-nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web)). In India, SEBI Circular No. HO/(411)2026-ITD-5_DIV2/I/17922/2026 of 31 Jul 2026 extended the timelines for its Digital Accessibility Circulars ([SEBI, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/jul-2026/extension-of-timelines-with-repect-to-compliance-of-digital-accessibility-circulars-_103277.html)).

**What Vol 2 has today.** The Turn 83 summary says only "keeping AI features accessible", and Turn 122 mentions "clean accessible HTML" for agent-readability, not disability access. Nothing found after searching for: "WCAG", "European Accessibility", "screen reader", "disab", "EN 301 549".

**What to teach.**
- Decide which interfaces are in scope: customer-facing chat for EU banking and e-commerce, US state and local government apps, and SEBI-regulated entities in India. Internal employee tools are outside the EAA.
- Make streaming work with screen readers. Use a polite live region that announces completed chunks or messages, not every token. Keep keyboard focus stable, label every control, and provide a stop-generation control.
- Give every step a keyboard path and a non-voice alternative for voice-only agents. Make CAPTCHAs and age or identity checks accessible.
- Test with assistive technology against WCAG 2.1 AA or EN 301 549 before launch, and write accessibility defects into acceptance criteria as legal defects, not polish.

**Idea to remember.** A chatbot on a bank's or shop's website is a regulated digital service. Stream text so screen readers can follow it, give every step a keyboard path and a non-voice path, and test with assistive technology before launch.

**Interview questions.**
1. What breaks accessibility in a streaming chat UI, and how do you fix it? — Announcing every token floods screen readers, focus jumps, and controls are unlabelled. Use a polite live region that announces completed chunks or messages, keep keyboard focus stable, label controls, and provide a stop-generation control.
2. Which laws make chatbot accessibility mandatory? — In the EU, the European Accessibility Act for customer-facing services such as banking and e-commerce (from 28 Jun 2025; microenterprises exempt; internal tools not covered). In the US, ADA Title II for state and local governments: WCAG 2.1 AA by 26 Apr 2027 for populations of 50,000 or more, and 26 Apr 2028 for smaller entities. In India, SEBI's digital-accessibility circulars for regulated entities, whose timelines were extended on 31 Jul 2026.

### SEC-14 · Outbound AI voice and text agents under US telemarketing law (TCPA and FCC AI-voice ruling)  — **P2** · THIN · Section H · extend Turn 60 (cross-link Turn 82)

*Overlaps gap doc:* none

**What it is.** The legal design constraints for AI agents that place calls or send texts. The US Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) treats AI-generated voices as an "artificial or prerecorded voice". Outbound AI calls therefore need prior express consent (prior express written consent for telemarketing), caller identification and opt-out handling, plus consent records that can be proven in litigation.

**Why now.** The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (adopted 2 Feb 2024, released 8 Feb 2024) confirms that "the TCPA's restrictions on the use of 'artificial or prerecorded voice' encompass current AI technologies that generate human voices". Such calls therefore "require the prior express consent of the called party ... absent an emergency purpose or exemption" ([FCC, 8 Feb 2024](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf); [Mayer Brown, Feb 2024](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/fcc-declares-authority-and-intent-to-regulate-ai-generated-calls-under-the-tcpa)). The FCC's 7 Aug 2024 notice of proposed rulemaking (FCC 24-84) proposed further AI-call disclosure rules ([FCC, Aug 2024](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-84A1.pdf)). The TCPA gives a private right of action of $500 per violation, which a court may treble for wilful or knowing violations (47 U.S.C. §227(b)(3)) [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227), so a badly scoped outbound voice pilot can become class-action exposure.

**What Vol 2 has today.** Turn 60 Q297 lists "AI disclosure, recording consent, transcript privacy, voice-cloning consent and a path to a human". Nothing found after searching for: "TCPA", "robocall", "telemarket", "outbound".

**What to teach.**
- Classify each outbound call or text campaign (telemarketing or not). Remember that an AI voice counts as an "artificial or prerecorded voice".
- Put a consent gate before the dialler. Check prior express consent for each number (prior express written consent if any call is telemarketing), and block the call otherwise, unless it has an emergency purpose or an exemption.
- Identify the caller, honour opt-outs immediately across channels, and keep a suppression list.
- Log consent evidence for each number (source, time, scope) in a form that can be proven in litigation.
- Track the FCC's proposed AI-call disclosure rules (FCC 24-84) as a proposal. Keep inbound lines under the disclosure, recording-consent and privacy rules in Turn 60.

**Idea to remember.** In the US, an AI voice is legally an "artificial voice": make no outbound AI call without provable prior consent, because every non-compliant call is a $500-$1,500 claim.

**Interview questions.**
1. A US lender wants your AI voice agent to call overdue borrowers. What legal constraint shapes the design? — Under the FCC's Feb 2024 ruling, AI-generated voices count as an "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the TCPA. The calls need the borrower's prior express consent, or prior express written consent if any call is telemarketing. Build consent checks before dialling, identify the caller, honour opt-outs immediately and log consent evidence for each number.
2. Why is TCPA non-compliance so expensive? — The statute gives a private right of action of $500 per call or text, which a court can treble for wilful or knowing violations. Multiplied across a campaign, this drives class actions.
3. Does the TCPA cover an inbound AI customer-service line? — Its artificial-voice restrictions target calls the business makes. An inbound line is governed instead by disclosure, recording-consent and privacy rules (Turn 60), plus any state bot-disclosure law.

### SEC-9 · Model extraction and distillation abuse: protecting your model APIs  — **P3** · MISSING · Section H · extend Turn 74 (the terms gate for distilling from someone else's API goes once in Turn 23, via MOD-13)

*Overlaps gap doc:* none

**What it is.** Attackers query an API at scale to copy a model's capabilities (distillation) or its parameters (extraction). Defences include per-account behavioural detection, limits on log-probabilities and hidden-state outputs, rate and volume caps, and detection of account networks.

**Why now.** On 23 Feb 2026 Anthropic reported industrial-scale distillation campaigns by DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and MiniMax: "over 16 million exchanges ... through approximately 24,000 fraudulent accounts". The campaigns used "hydra cluster" proxy networks and prompts that elicit chain-of-thought training data; the rubric-grading "reward model" technique is attributed specifically to DeepSeek ([Anthropic, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)). Carlini et al. extracted the embedding-projection matrices of OpenAI's Ada and Babbage models for under US$20 ([arXiv 2403.06634, 11 Mar 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06634)). Commercial terms restrict the practice: Anthropic's Commercial Terms D.4 bar using the services "to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models" ([Anthropic Commercial Terms, accessed 26 Sep 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)).

**What Vol 2 has today.** Turn 23 Q110 presents sequence-level distillation neutrally ("it works even when only a text API is available"), and Turn 74 Q367 covers "denial of wallet" abuse but not capability extraction. Nothing found after searching for: "model stealing", "extraction attack", "distillation attack", "terms of service".

**What to teach.**
- Recognise extraction-shaped traffic: high volumes of systematically varied prompts, prompts that elicit step-by-step reasoning or rubric grading, synchronised accounts that share payment methods or infrastructure, and proxy resellers.
- Limit what the API exposes. Don't return log-probabilities or hidden states unless a use case needs them, and cap rate and volume per account and per account network.
- Respond at the level of the account network ("hydra cluster"), not the single key, because banned keys are cheap to replace.
- Before distilling from someone else's API, run the terms gate in Turn 23 (MOD-13).

**Idea to remember.** An API is also a copying channel. Watch for extraction-shaped traffic and limit log-probabilities, and read the provider's terms before you distil from someone else's API.

**Interview questions.**
1. How do you detect distillation traffic against your model API? — Look for extraction-shaped usage: high volumes of systematically varied prompts, requests that elicit step-by-step reasoning or rubric grading, synchronised accounts that share payment methods or infrastructure, and proxy resellers. Respond with account-level and network-level limits and bans, and don't expose log-probabilities you don't need to.
2. Can we distil a small model from a commercial API's outputs? — Terms commonly forbid training models that compete with the provider. Whether a small internal task model counts as competing is a contract question, so check the specific terms and get written approval, or use a permissively licensed open-weight teacher.

Rewrite Q406 around SR 26-2 and the RBI draft, and add NIS2 and CRA pointers.
